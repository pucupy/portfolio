03: CASE STUDY

Turning a sales-led onboarding into a self-serve one

The Console is where Yapily customers sign up, manage applications and configure their integration.

As the company grew, the product didn’t keep up. Anything the Console couldn’t do became manual work for support, engineering or customer success. Becoming a customer could take six months to a year.

The problem

The Console had originally been built for a handful of enterprise customers.

Years later, around 80% of customers were non-enterprise, but everyone still went through the same sales-led onboarding.

The cost showed up in people’s time.

Support enabled features manually. Engineers changed configuration. Customer success exported reports. Almost every stage depended on somebody being available.

Making the problem visible

The Console wasn’t on the roadmap, so the first job was making the case for investing in it.

I ran:

  1. 01customer interviews
  2. 02a cross-functional workshop with product, engineering, support and customer success
  3. 03a two-day hackathon where we tried to onboard using only what a real customer could access
  4. 04an end-to-end map from first website visit to renewal

The workshop changed the conversation. What had looked like a design complaint became a visible operational cost across several teams.

The Console was prioritised.

The strategy

Instead of forcing every customer through one process, we split the path in two.

Enterprise customers keep an assisted experience.

Everyone else gets self-service: create an account, access a sandbox, complete verification, configure products and move towards production without waiting for meetings.

The target is to reduce onboarding from six months–one year to weeks–a few months.

What changed

Customers can now:

  1. 01create an organisation and sandbox immediately
  2. 02generate applications and credentials
  3. 03invite team members and assign roles
  4. 04complete KYB online
  5. 05manage bank coverage
  6. 06configure Hosted Pages
  7. 07see analytics, billing and integration status directly

The same Console serves evaluation, onboarding and production instead of handing customers between disconnected tools and teams.

What shipped

The Hosted Pages configurator is already live.

Previously, changing branding or configuration required a support ticket and an engineer. Customers can now manage it themselves, removing that manual work entirely.

The wider Console is currently in beta, so it is too early to report adoption or final onboarding metrics.

What I learned

The biggest issue wasn’t the UI. It was that the product had been designed around one customer type and one way of working.

The recurring question I now ask much earlier is:

Can there be more than one of this?

One customer type. One licence. One organisation. One behaviour.

Asking that question early is cheap. Retrofitting the answer years later isn’t.
