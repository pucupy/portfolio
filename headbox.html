06: CASE STUDY

Turning a marketplace the right way round

My clearest example of research changing a business model, not just a screen.

£150,228ARR in three months · 117 paying venues · 4× the previous model

At a glance

Period
December 2018: December 2019
Company
HeadBox, enterprise event technology
Working with
Sales, account management
Status
Shipped; the company pivoted its business model on the result

The problem

HeadBox was a two-sided marketplace for event spaces. Guests searched a directory, contacted a few venues, then often left the platform and called them directly.

Hosts had the opposite problem: too many poorly matched enquiries, so genuine leads were buried. Only 20% received a proposal.

Around 80% of the company was sales and account management, manually matching guests and venues.

I was asked to improve conversion. The assumption was that we needed better search and matching. Research showed the marketplace itself was the problem.

What I found

I ran three rounds of research with 45 customers and non-customers, alongside usability testing and internal research.

The expertise was on the wrong side.

Guests booked events occasionally but were being asked to choose like experts. Hosts did this every day but were given an unfocused queue of enquiries.

Instead of improving the directory, I proposed reversing the model.

The idea

  1. 01Guests describe what they need through a short wizard.
  2. 02Those briefs enter a central feed.
  3. 03Hosts choose the events they want to respond to.

The revenue model changed with it: venues paid for access to the feed instead of HeadBox taking commission from bookings.

Testing before building

We tested each assumption as cheaply as possible.

The first host feed was a spreadsheet shown to 20 venues. Eighteen of the 20 events became confirmed bookings.

The guest flow began as a Google Form before moving through Figma and coded prototypes.

Venue listings were also rebuilt around structured capacity, layout and suitability data so briefs could be matched reliably.

Result

Within three months:

  1. 01£150,228 ARR
  2. 02117 paying venues
  3. 034× the previous revenue model
  4. 041,500+ leads in the first month
  5. 05Brief completion increased from roughly 50% to 75%
  6. 06Hosts logged in every day from launch

The company pivoted its business model around the result.

The core model still exists today, and the wizard remains HeadBox’s main entry point in 2026.

What I took from it

Every previous attempt asked how to make the existing marketplace work harder.

The successful one asked a different question: who should be doing the difficult part?

Moving that work from occasional guests to expert hosts changed the product, the revenue model and ultimately the business.
